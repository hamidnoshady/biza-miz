# Phase 16 — Real Accounting Suite

**Project:** Cafe/Restaurant POS
**Depends on:** Phase 12 (tenant-safe ledger), Phase 13 (`accountant` role)
**Goal:** Turn the existing double-entry ledger into an accounting system an accountant can actually close a year on.

---

## Scope

What exists after Phase 7: a double-entry ledger, a Persian F&B chart of accounts, auto-posting for payments, purchases, COGS and waste, and a trial balance. What this phase adds:

- **Fiscal years and periods** — define them per business, soft-close and hard-lock a period so nothing can post into it, with a controlled reopen. Year-end closing entries rolling P&L into retained earnings.
- **Financial statements** — profit & loss, balance sheet, and cash flow, each with period comparison and drill-down to the journal entries behind any figure.
- **AR subledger** — customer balances, invoices, receipts, aging buckets, statements. `accounts_receivable` is posted to today but has no subledger behind it.
- **AP subledger** — supplier balances, bills against purchase receipts, payments, aging, statements.
- **Bank & cash reconciliation** — import or enter a statement, match against `cash` / `bank` / `bankClearing` postings, carry unreconciled items, lock a reconciled period.
- **Expense management** — categorised operating expenses with attachments, recurring expenses, and their postings.
- **Payroll entries** — staff cost accrual and payment postings (journal-level, not a payroll engine).
- **Manual journals, properly** — draft → review → post workflow, reversal rather than deletion, recurring templates, and an approval permission distinct from posting.
- **Per-business chart of accounts** — customisation, sub-accounts, and account archival that respects existing postings.
- **VAT / tax reporting** — output vs input VAT, payable position, and a return-shaped report.

## Out of scope

- Iranian statutory e-invoicing (سامانه مودیان) and official ledger exports — a large integration in its own right; revisit after this lands.
- Multi-currency and FX revaluation — the storage convention is integer Rial throughout and changing that touches every money path in the system.
- A payroll engine (tax tables, insurance, payslips).

## Exit criteria

- A full fiscal year can be closed: periods locked, closing entries posted, statements produced, and the balance sheet balances.
- P&L and balance sheet agree with the trial balance, and every figure drills through to its journal lines.
- AR and AP aging totals agree with their control accounts to the Rial.
- A locked period rejects every posting path, including the automatic ones (order payment, purchase receipt, waste, inventory adjustment).
- Reversing an entry leaves both the original and the reversal visible and the net effect zero.

## Open questions → decisions

1. **Fiscal year** — the Jalali year (فروردین–اسفند), no configurable start month. Simplest default; revisit only if a business genuinely needs otherwise.
2. **AR invoices** — no new document type. An order with `credit` payment method is the invoice; the AR subledger reads off orders already marked credit.
3. **Closed-but-not-locked posting** — owner and accountant, not accountant-only. Matches `ledger.close_period`'s existing role preset (see `permissions.ts`).
4. **Statement format** — a clean internal format for now; statutory Persian formatting (already out of scope alongside e-invoicing) can follow later.
5. **Bank reconciliation import** — manual entry first; a bank-file import format is deferred until reconciliation itself is built.
6. **AR customer attribution** — orders had a `customer_id` column since Phase 1 but nothing populated it. Checkout now requires picking or creating a customer when the payment method is `credit`, so the subledger has a real entity to group by going forward; existing/legacy credit orders with no customer stay grouped under an explicit "unknown" bucket rather than being dropped, so totals still agree with the control account to the Rial.
7. **AR collection** — built now, not deferred: a credit order posts as fully "settled" the instant it's rung up (no partial-payment order state), so without a way to record the customer later paying it down, the subledger could only ever grow. `ar_receipts` + `receivePayment` (`ar-service.ts`) is that missing other half.
8. **AP supplier attribution** — unlike orders, `purchases.supplier_id` was already populated when set, but selection was (and stays) optional at purchase creation. A supplier is instead required only at the point it starts to matter: receiving a purchase with the `credit` settlement method now requires a supplier, either already on the purchase or supplied at that point — mirroring how AR asks for a customer at the credit decision, not earlier.
9. **AP collection** — same reasoning as AR collection: `ap_payments` + `payBill` (`ap-service.ts`).
10. **"Lock a reconciled period" is its own concept, not the fiscal-period lock.** Migration 0024's trigger blocks *posting* into a date range, business-wide, on every account. Reconciliation locks specific *lines* on one specific account (cash or bank-clearing) as matched — orthogonal to whether the fiscal period covering their dates is open, soft-closed, or already locked, and it must keep working regardless.
11. **Drafts live in a separate table, not a `status` column on `journal_entries`.** Migration 0024's lock trigger fires on every insert into `journal_entries` unconditionally, and every report/subledger/reconciliation query built so far reads that table with no status filter — a `draft` status there would have meant the trigger wrongly rejecting draft inserts dated in a locked period, plus auditing every one of those queries. A draft has zero financial effect until approved, so it isn't a journal entry yet. Approving one calls the same `postJournalEntry()` every other posting path uses, at exactly the point a real posting is created.
12. **Reversal always posts today (or an explicit date), never backdated into the original's period.** "You don't reopen a closed period to fix a mistake, you post a reversing entry now" — so `reverseEntry` reuses `postJournalEntry()` (and therefore the fiscal-period trigger) exactly like a fresh manual entry, rather than trying to insert alongside the original.
13. **`ledger.approve` gates both draft-approval and reversal**, distinct from the role list that gates drafting (owner/manager/accountant). It was already defined and granted to `accountant` by preset since Phase 13 but wired to nothing; this is its first use. No self-approval restriction — the same accountant who drafted an entry may also approve it, matching how the rest of this phase's role-based (not maker-checker) surfaces work. Rejecting a draft is the one exception: the creator may always withdraw their own draft, or anyone holding `ledger.approve` may reject someone else's.
14. **Recurring templates deferred, not built in this slice.** The phase scope bullet lists it alongside the draft/review/post workflow and reversal, but nothing in the exit criteria depends on it; revisit if a real recurring-entry need shows up (e.g. once expense management's recurring expenses land, the two may want to share a scheduler rather than each inventing one).
15. **Archiving an account never touches its history; only deletion is restricted by it.** `accounts.parent_id`/`is_active` have existed since Phase 1 with no CRUD ever built on top. Archiving just flips `is_active` — every past trial balance, statement, and drill-down still shows the account's postings exactly as before, and the only new effect is that it's no longer offered for *new* ones (the manual-journal draft/approve path now filters `is_active`, the same way the account picker already did). Deletion is the opposite: irreversible, so it's only ever allowed for an account with zero postings (real or drafted) and no sub-accounts — otherwise archive it instead. A well-known code (anything `postJournalEntry`/auto-posting looks up by code — cash, VAT payable, retained earnings, …) can never be archived or deleted, full stop, regardless of postings.
16. **An expense is recorded as paid, not as a bill owed.** The AP subledger already models "money owed to a specific supplier" for purchases; genericising that to also cover "rent got paid today" would blur two different things it's cleaner to keep separate. A business paying an operating expense on credit can still post that through the manual-journal workflow against Accounts Payable directly. **"Categorised" needs no new taxonomy**: the expense account chosen (any active `type='expense'` account — the default chart's 5200-5900, or however chart-of-accounts customisation has reshaped it) *is* the category, so there's exactly one place a business manages its expense categories rather than two. **Attachments and recurring expenses are deferred** — no attachment infrastructure exists anywhere in the codebase yet (the only file-upload-adjacent code, `s3-lite.ts`, is backup-specific and assumes per-business S3 config most businesses won't have configured for anything else), and recurring was already deferred once for manual journals (decision 14) for the same reason: nothing in the exit criteria depends on either, and a future recurring-entry scheduler should probably serve both needs at once rather than each slice inventing its own.
17. **Payroll access is owner + accountant only, narrower than every other subledger in this phase** — asked of the user rather than decided unilaterally, since it's a real product trade-off (managers can post everywhere else in this phase) rather than an implementation detail. Wages are compensation data, more sensitive than an ordinary ledger entry; a manager who can post a manual journal or reconcile the bank account still shouldn't see or set what a coworker earns. The "حقوق و دستمزد" tab is hidden entirely from managers (not just left to 403 on click, the way approve/reverse buttons are shown-then-rejected elsewhere in this phase) — showing a broken tab for salary data seemed worse than not showing it, given the sensitivity.
18. **A payroll run accrues against a snapshot, not a live formula.** `payroll_run_lines` copies each staff member's `monthly_wage` at the moment of accrual; changing a wage later never rewrites a past run's total, the same "history doesn't move" principle as everything else in this phase's ledger. Two postings, not one: accrual (Debit salariesExpense 5200 / Credit salariesPayable 2300) records the cost when it's incurred; payment (Debit salariesPayable / Credit Cash/Bank-Clearing) records when it's actually paid out — mirroring the AR/AP two-step (invoice, then collect/pay) rather than expense management's single "paid immediately" posting, because unlike a rent bill, payroll is normally accrued (the pay period ends) before it's actually disbursed (payday).
19. **Input VAT is recorded via the existing manual-journal workflow, not built into purchase receiving** — asked of the user rather than decided unilaterally, since it's a real trade-off between two working designs, not an implementation detail. Output VAT was already fully wired (`vatPayable`, credited on every order payment since Phase 7); input VAT had no account or posting path at all. The alternative — an optional VAT field on the purchase-receiving screen, auto-splitting one posting into inventory-net-of-VAT plus a VAT line — would have touched `ledger-service.ts`'s core purchase-posting path, the same code inventory costing and COGS depend on. The chosen design (a new `vatReceivable` well-known account, recorded via a second, separate manual-journal entry alongside entering a supplier bill) keeps purchase receiving completely untouched at the cost of one extra manual step per purchase with input VAT to claim.

- **VAT/tax reporting — implemented.** Output VAT needed nothing new (`vatPayable`, 2200, has posted on every order payment since Phase 7). Input VAT gets a new well-known account, `vatReceivable` (1220, `migrations/0032_vat_reporting.sql`, backfilled onto every existing business the same way migration 0025 backfilled Retained Earnings) — recorded as its own manual-journal entry against it (decision 19), deliberately not built into purchase receiving. `getVatReport` (`reports-service.ts`) reads both control accounts directly off `journal_lines`/`journal_entries` for a given period: `outputVat` (net credit movement on `vatPayable`), `inputVat` (net debit movement on `vatReceivable`), `netPayable = outputVat - inputVat` (positive owed to the tax authority, negative refundable), plus each account's all-time cumulative balance as of the period's end (not reset by narrowing the reported period) — the same "compute straight from the ledger, never a shadow copy" discipline as P&L/Balance Sheet/Cash Flow. Settling the position with the tax authority is just another manual-journal entry against `vatPayable`, same as any other account settlement in this phase. Exposed via `GET /api/ledger/vat` (`requireRole("owner","manager","accountant")`, the standard ledger-view access — nothing about this report is more sensitive than the rest of the ledger, unlike payroll) and a "گزارش مالیات" tab (a date-range picker, the three headline figures, and both accounts' cumulative balances) — built as a `ledger-manager.tsx` tab rather than through the generic `/accounting/reports` "standard reports" framework (which bundles chart rendering, CSV/PDF export, and dashboard-pinning that nothing here needs), consistent with how every other Phase 16 feature this phase added its own dedicated ledger tab instead. Verified in `integration/vat-report.integration.test.ts` (zero-activity baseline, netting output against input for postings in vs. out of the reported period, the payable-vs-refundable sign flip, and the cumulative balance staying keyed to `dateTo` regardless of a narrower `dateFrom`) and manually end-to-end (posted an output-VAT-bearing sale directly, recorded input VAT through the manual-journal workflow, confirmed the VAT report tab showed the exact output/input/net-payable figures and matching cumulative balances) against a real server.
